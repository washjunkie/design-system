A white mark on brand teal is not legible
It is the combination everyone reaches for first, and a brand-teal van with a white wordmark washes out at distance and disappears in direct sun. The line that survives being passed on by phone: ink on the bright teal, white on the deep teal, never white on the bright teal.

Clear space & geometry

Clear space is 0.45 × height on all four sides — the height of the splash mark's own droplet, so the space around the artwork is set by a shape inside it. Nothing enters that box: not a rule, not a partner logo, not a fold, not a seam.

Mark · keep-clear box 2.920H × 1.9H

A lock-up 40mm tall is 232mm wide, carries 18mm of clear space, and needs a panel of at least 268 × 76mm with nothing else in it. Size by height, never width.

The lock-up is not embroiderable at chest-badge size
A 70mm-wide lock-up is 12mm tall and its thinnest stroke 0.98mm — under the satin floor. Below 23mm, embroider the mark. Below 23mm, do not cut vinyl — print and cut.

Reproduction fidelity is not legibility. Below about 5mm a lock-up is holdable detail but unreadable at arm's length whatever the press can do. The feature figures are typical working values, not specifications — TO SPECIFY with each supplier.

App icon & favicon — one number for three masks

Always the mark, never the lock-up: every icon slot is square or round, and 5.804 : 1 does not fit in one. Mark height = 0.25 × the icon side, centred. At 2.020 : 1 that is 0.505 × the side wide and 0.564 across the diagonal — clearing Android's 0.61 guaranteed circle. The geometric ceiling is 0.27.

The instinctive app icon is the one that fails
A brand-teal square with a white mark is the 2.26:1 case, at exactly the size where it gets one glance. Use an ink mark on paper, or a white mark on ink or accent teal. Note also that currentColor does not resolve in a favicon — there is no inheriting context, so the raw asset's own fill renders, and the tab strip is light or dark by OS theme, not by anything you control.

Height by viewing distance

"Make it big" is not a spec. The eye resolves about one arcminute, so a feature of width w is at threshold at w = D / 3438. The signwriter's margin of cap = D / 120 puts a working stroke at D / 800 — 4.3 × threshold, which is the allowance for movement, glare, and a reader who is not staring.

The van, panel by panel

A van is the brand's largest surface and its least controlled one: seen moving, from behind, at an angle, in sun and at night, parked against anything.

A wrap is not a print

  • Curves, swage lines and wheel arches stretch the film locally — the mark needs a flat panel, with its clear space inside the largest flat rectangle.
  • Rivets and recesses are heat-formed; artwork crossing them dimples and shifts colour where the film thins.
  • Never cross a shut-line. A split mark misregisters every time the door opens, and permanently once a panel is replaced after a knock.
  • Cast, not calendered. Factory-coloured film beats printed film for the teal, and matte beats gloss on anything that must be read — the reflection kills legibility before the colour does.
Proposal — needs sign-off
The scheme above is derived from the contrast constraint. The constraint is fact; the scheme is not yet a decision. Rear conspicuity marking is a legal requirement in many markets and belongs to that market's regulator — confirm it before the wrap is designed, because it claims a fixed band of the rear panel.

The driver decision does not settle this: a vehicle is an object, and a provider's van is the artefact question. But note the cab-door pair sits at 2–5m at the kerb, beside an unmarked driver — it is the same claim in a larger size, and that should be decided knowing so.

The brand teal is in the hardest region for process ink
Saturated cyan-green above 60% lightness is the largest part of sRGB that offset CMYK cannot reach. It will flatten toward grey-green. Anything where the teal is the hero needs a spot colour.

Five colours, one grey

Five of the eight brand colours sit inside a 0.031 luminance band. Moss against ember is 1.16:1; moss against sun is 1.08:1. In greyscale they are one grey; in 1-bit they are all black.

Photocopier · thermal printer · single-colour screen print
This is why status is never colour alone
Any artefact relying on telling two colours apart — a rack tag, a route label, a status sticker — must carry a word or a shape too, or it stops working the moment it is photocopied. The same rule the product enforces with colour + dot + word, hitting paper.

The thermal receipt, at size

58mm roll, 203dpi, 1-bit. A dot is 0.125mm and a 58mm roll is commonly 384 dots ≈ 48mm of print width. There are no greys to fall back on — the mock below is rendered through a real 1-bit filter, not drawn to look like one.

  • 3mm cap height minimum for anything a customer reads. Reading distance ≈ 350mm, so cap = D / 120 gives 2.9mm. At 3mm the stroke is 0.45mm ≈ 3.6 dots, comfortably over the 2-dot survival floor.
  • 2mm is the absolute floor, secondary lines only. At 2mm the stroke is 2.4 dots — one dot of head bleed and it closes.
  • Thermal floors: mark 5.5mm (44 × 89 dots), lock-up 6mm (48 × 278 dots).
  • The reference is the largest thing after the total. Seven-character Crockford base32 — digits plus A–Z with I, L, O, U removed. Crockford keeps 5/S and 2/Z, so the face does the rest — set it in JetBrains Mono.
  • Money uses the ISO code (NGN 1,200), not the symbol — the ₦ glyph is missing from the fonts that ship with most receipt and PDF pipelines.
  • Artwork ships as a 1-bit bitmap at the device's own resolution, never a scaled greyscale.
  • Thermal paper fades. It is a receipt, not a record.
  • No platform mark on this document — the business leads. Not deference: at the thermal mark's 5.5mm floor its keep-clear box is 16.1 × 10.5mm, a third of a 58mm roll's 48mm print width, competing with the order reference — and the reference outranks the mark. Wash Junkie appears as one line of type in the footer, which is also the only form the shipping code should ever render.

Writing rules

Who may act as Wash Junkie

Chapter 8 governs who may use the marks. This governs who may act as the company — which for a platform whose field agents walk into a stranger's business and ask for control of their account is the higher-stakes question.

PersonRepresentsMay sayNever says
Three asks mean it is not us
A Wash Junkie representative never asks for a password, never asks for a sign-in code out of your inbox, and never takes money. One sentence a provider can carry without training — and it costs nothing, because sign-in is Google or an emailed link, the owner claims the account in their own inbox, and funding is owner-only.

It runs back into the product as a hard constraint: no flow may ever ask an agent to type a code that arrived in the owner's inbox. The day one does, the sentence stops being true and every provider who learned it has been taught to trust the next impostor.

The identification test

Can the provider verify the agent using something the agent did not give them? If the only proof is on the agent's body or in the agent's hand, it is not proof.

The driver question — decided

A driver is the most visible person in the service and the surface a brand most wants — and it is not ours to take. Driver is a record under a Business; the platform does not engage, pay or vet the person. A mark in our livery at a door says Wash Junkie collected your clothes, the one sentence the guide bans outright. And one person may hold driver records at two providers and work both in a day.

Decided — option A. Platform-supplied kit carries no platform mark on a driver.
The derivation is standing, not surface: an endorsement may sit on any surface whose owner can stand behind what it says, and a provider's driver cannot — the garment would name a party that neither did the work nor answers for it. There is no general rule that endorsement cannot go on a person; it goes on a field agent.

Scope: kit we specify, supply or catalogue. A provider printing our name in plain text on their own driver's shirt is their existing grant. Reversal: reopens — as a fresh derivation across A, B and C, not as C — the moment Wash Junkie engages, pays, vets, schedules or disciplines a driver directly, or contracts a fleet serving more than one provider. Not time-boxed, and not tied to the Customer App.
The debt it takes on, stated honestly
A customer should be able to tell that the person at the door is the one they were told to expect — and today nothing tells them. The only customer-facing notice is the per-business order-status SMS, which names the business and the reference and nothing about who is coming. It is partly dischargeable now (that service already resolves a first name), and nobody owns it. Until someone does, this is a debt, not a plan.

Merchandise

An item earns the mark by having a job in the service. If you cannot name the moment in an order's life when someone uses it, it is not merchandise — it is landfill with our name on it.

What Wash Junkie is not
