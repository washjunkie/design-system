# Merchandise

*For whoever is about to order something with the mark on it — a bag, a garment,
a shopfront kit, a stand — and for whoever has to say no. Open it before you ask
a supplier for a quote, and again when the sample arrives.*

The gate is set in [Physical
applications](06-physical-applications.md#merchandise) and is not restated
differently here:

> **An item earns the mark by having a job in the service.** If you cannot name
> the moment in an order's life when someone uses it, it is not merchandise — it
> is landfill with our name on it.

That test decides *whether*. This document is the **programme**: which audience
each item serves, what it is made of, which pairing and floor it must clear, what
it costs to keep supplying, and who hands it to whom. Nothing physical has been
produced yet ([strategy open decision #10](01-strategy.md#open-decisions)), which
is why a programme is still cheaper to design than to unwind.

Three things decide every item below, in this order.

| | Question | Where the answer comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Does it have a job in an order's life? | The earning test above. One sentence, written down, before a quote is requested |
| **2** | What field is it, and what mark goes on it? | The measured table in [03 · which mark on which field](03-identity-system.md#which-mark-on-which-field). **The field is chosen first; the item is found second** |
| **3** | Can its flat panel hold the mark's floor *and* its clear space? | The process table in [03 · minimum sizes](03-identity-system.md#minimum-sizes-for-physical-reproduction), applied to the panel, not the object |

That order is the whole discipline. Reversed — item first, colour second, size
third — you arrive at a sample that cannot be fixed without re-tooling, having
already paid a deposit.

## The refusal people argue with: 2.26:1 by construction

Worth doing once, properly, because it is the ground contested in every quote
meeting. A promotional-goods catalogue is organised by **object and colour**,
never by contrast, and two ordinary facts collide in it:

1. The item comes in a stock colour range, and a supplier asked to match the
   brand matches **brand teal `#2cbec5`** — the hex on the website and the first
   swatch in the file.
2. The decoration that item supports is **one colour**, and on a mid-to-dark body
   that colour is **white**.

Neither party has done anything wrong, and the result is a **white mark on brand
teal: 2.26:1**, under the 3:1 non-text floor, on every unit in the run. Not a
judgement call and not fixable in artwork — a property of two numbers decided
before anyone looked at a proof. The four counter-arguments:

| The argument | The answer |
|---|---|
| *"It looks fine on the supplier's mock-up."* | A mock-up is a vector render on a backlit screen at high zoom. That is the single condition under which 2.26:1 is tolerable. The item will be seen on a shoulder across a room, in sun, at a glance |
| *"It's merch, not signage — the distance rule doesn't apply."* | Merchandise is read by people who are not holding it. A tote, a polo back, a bag by a front door: all read beyond arm's length, all inside the **4.5:1** rule for distance work ([03](03-identity-system.md#which-mark-on-which-field)) — which is our own floor, not a conformance standard, and must not be quoted to a supplier as one |
| *"Outline the white mark, or add a shadow."* | Outlining, shadowing and redrawing are [explicit misuse](03-identity-system.md#misuse). An outline also changes the stroke and gap ratios every floor in this guide is derived from, so the floor you were given no longer describes the artwork you are printing |
| *"Use a deeper teal, then."* | **Yes — that is the actual fix.** White on accent teal `#007e84` is 4.86:1. If the supplier's stock range does not hold something close to `#007e84`, the item is refused **on its colour range**, which is a supplier fact, not a brand opinion |

The escapes on a **coloured body**: **ink on brand teal, 7.24** · **white on
accent teal, 4.86** · **white on ink, 16.38** — and on ink, a **brand-teal mark,
also 7.24**. On a **light body** it is **ink, 15.98**. That is the whole approved
set an object can be quoted against
([03](03-identity-system.md#which-mark-on-which-field)). Two pairings sit outside
it and neither belongs on merchandise, because you do not control how far away an
object is read: a brand-teal mark on paper at **2.21**, carried at reading
distance only by the logotype exemption in 03, and an ink mark on accent teal at
**3.37**, which clears the 3:1 graphic floor but not our 4.5:1 distance rule and
is not on 03's approved list at all. The sentence that survives being passed on
by phone is the one the rest of the guide uses: *ink on the bright teal, white on
the deep teal, never white on the bright teal.*

## The four audiences

What earns shelf space in a shop is not what earns a place in a driver's bag.

| Audience | Where the object lives | What earns a place | What fails here specifically |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Provider owners and staff** | The shop floor, all day, for years | Something used in the work: the shopfront kit on go-live day, an apron that survives the room | Anything that competes with their own fascia. On their premises **their brand leads** ([08](08-usage-and-legal.md#whose-brand-leads-one-principle-two-media)); an item that makes the shop look like a branch of ours misstates the business model |
| **Drivers** | Outdoors, moving, in weather | Something that works wet and **one-handed** — that is the Driver App's whole design constraint and any object a driver carries inherits it | Anything needing two hands, a free pocket, or dry conditions. A driver is **the provider's, not ours**, and carries **no mark of ours at all** ([09](09-brand-representation.md#decided--option-a)) — so this audience is one we design *for* and never brand |
| **Customers** | A home, between orders | **Exactly one item: the laundry bag.** It is the only object with a job in a customer's part of an order | Everything else, and not for cost reasons. A customer's relationship is with the **provider**; a customer in our t-shirt is advertising a relationship with the wrong party |
| **Internal and events** | A stranger's first physical contact with the company | Something carrying information a stranger can act on — a card, a stand | A gift. Someone who has never used the platform has no job for a bag, so handing them one converts an earned object into a giveaway and teaches everyone watching that it is one |

The customer row surprises people, so say it plainly: **a one-item customer
catalogue is the correct answer, not an underfunded one.**

### The floor is not the panel

A floor is the mark's own height. What the object has to give up is the
**keep-clear box** — `2.920H × 1.9H` for the mark and `6.704H × 1.9H` for the
lock-up, each its own width ratio plus `0.45H` of clear space on four sides
([03](03-identity-system.md#clear-space-and-screen-minimums)).
Multiply it out and the argument ends:

| At its floor | The mark claims | The lock-up claims |
|---|---|---|
| **Embroidery** (22 / 23mm) | **64 × 42mm** of flat, unseamed panel | **154 × 44mm** — which is why the lock-up is a back panel or nothing |
| **Vinyl cut** (22 / 23mm) | 64 × 42mm | 154 × 44mm |
| **Screen print, textile** (11 / 12mm) | 32 × 21mm | 80 × 23mm |

That arithmetic is derived from ratios already published in
[03](03-identity-system.md#clear-space-and-screen-minimums) — not a new
specification, and the supplier's own minimum-feature figures still outrank it.
Its use is at the quote stage: hold 64 × 42mm against a cap side panel, a pen
barrel or a keyring fob and most of a promotional catalogue disqualifies itself
before anyone draws anything.

## What we refuse, and why

Four grounds follow from [the earning
test](06-physical-applications.md#merchandise); any one is enough on its own.

| Ground | The argument |
|---|---|
| It is **disposable** | A branded pen argues that we treat objects as disposable, in the exact medium we are asking to be trusted with |
| Its surface **forces a failing pairing** | Typically an item that only takes white printing and only comes in brand teal. That is 2.26:1 by construction, and wanting it does not change the number |
| It **cannot hold the mark's process floor** | A 15mm-wide item cannot carry a 22mm embroidered mark, and shrinking it anyway produces a smudge on four thousand units |
| We **cannot control its quality** | A cheap item in someone's hand is a physical argument about how carefully we would handle their clothes |

Worked examples, because the grounds are agreed in the abstract and argued in the
specific:

| Asked for | Refused on | The specific fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Branded pens** | Disposable | Also a curved barrel: every floor in this guide assumes a flat panel, and a pad print on a curve is neither flat nor measurable against one |
| **Teal drinkware, white mark** | Failing pairing | 2.26:1, the case argued above. Ask the supplier for a body near `#007e84`; if the range does not hold one, the item is out |
| **Keyring fobs** | Cannot hold the floor | Not the mark — the **box**. A 15mm fob laser-engraved carries a 7mm mark, over the 6.5mm laser floor, but its keep-clear box is `2.920 × 7` = **20mm wide on a 15mm object** |
| **Umbrellas** | Earning test | A driver has **one free hand**. An object that occupies the other one cannot have a job in a pickup. And the driver is standing in the rain on the **provider's** behalf, not ours ([09](09-brand-representation.md#decided--option-a)) — so it is not our object to give |
| **Customer t-shirts, caps, hoodies** | Earning test | A customer's relationship is with the provider. Dressing customers as us contradicts the endorsement principle in the most public medium available ([08](08-usage-and-legal.md#whose-brand-leads-one-principle-two-media)) |
| **Branded hangers** | Earning test, and a factual error | A marked hanger on a finished garment says the platform finished the garment. **Wash Junkie does not wash anything** — the provider washes, and the liability follows the work |
| **Branded detergent, sachets, any consumable that touches a garment** | Earning test, and a factual error | Same misassignment, plus a product liability we would be inventing for ourselves. Route anything of this shape to legal, not to a supplier |
| **Notebooks, laptop stickers, lanyards other than agent kit** | Not merchandise at all | These are office supplies. They are outside this programme, and the only rule they carry is that nothing leaves the building on an unapproved pairing. An agent's lanyard is kit, not merchandise, and [09](09-brand-representation.md#identification) is clear about what it is worth: recognition, never evidence |
| **Anything sold to anyone** | Governed elsewhere | Selling an item carrying the mark needs a **written agreement** ([08](08-usage-and-legal.md#third-party-usage)) — including a run sold at cost to a provider |

Two standing rules survive every exception. **Nothing durable carries a phone
number** ([06](06-physical-applications.md#merchandise)) — put the URL on it and
nothing else, because a URL survives an office move and five hundred printed bags
do not. The service card sits outside that rule only because it is paper and
reprinted; what may actually appear on it is
[09's](09-brand-representation.md#what-an-agent-leaves-behind), and an agent's own
number is not on the list. And
**nothing carries a person's name or a role claim**: people leave, and a garment
reading "Wash Junkie Driver" was wrong on the day it was stitched, because
drivers belong to the provider ([09](09-brand-representation.md)).

## Quality, and how it is checked

The fourth refusal ground quietly decides the programme's cost, so it needs a
floor and a method rather than an adjective. **The floor:** an item must survive
the treatment the service it advertises implies. For anything that meets laundry,
that means a wash — at the hottest programme the outlet actually runs, not a
temperature from a textile standard we have not verified. **TO SPECIFY:** the
cycle count and the temperature, from a real Wash House programme.

| Check | Method | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **Wash test** | Sample through the wash, then read the artwork | The whole quality claim of a laundry brand is legible on an object that has been washed. Print cracks, transfers lift, thread does not — 06's uniform table is the same finding on a different object |
| **Load test on anything with a handle** | Static load at the bag's rated capacity, held | The failure mode is a handle tearing out of a full bag on a doorstep — the worst brand moment available to us ([06](06-physical-applications.md#laundry-bags)). Rated capacity depends on the bag size, still open as [06 #7](06-physical-applications.md#open-decisions-and-to-specify) |
| **A week in the job** | One sample carried by someone doing the actual work for a week before any run is committed | Cheap, and it catches what no specification does: a cord lock that needs two hands, a strap that slips off a shoulder, a colour that shows every splash |
| **The photograph test** | Photograph the sample badly, in a shop, on a phone | Someone will. If the item only looks right in the supplier's studio render, the item is the problem |
| **Never from a picture** | A sample in a hand, or nothing | **Nobody signs off a physical piece on a screen** ([06](06-physical-applications.md#before-any-physical-order-ships)) — a screen cannot show thread on polyester or ink on canvas |

And the quantity rule, a quality control disguised as a purchasing one: **a
supplier's minimum order quantity is a reason to refuse an unproven item, not a
reason to order four thousand of it.** Order the smallest run that survives the
tests, then reorder. An item that only makes economic sense at a quantity we
cannot test is not affordable; it is unpriced.

## Sustainability and end of life

Honest, or silent. No third option survives contact with a regulator.

**What we can say**, because we control it and it is a design fact:

- The bag is **woven, not non-woven** — it does not delaminate and it can be
  repaired ([06](06-physical-applications.md#laundry-bags)).
- It is designed to be **refilled**, and the order reference sits on a
  replaceable label rather than printed on, so the bag outlives the order.
- **The most environmental decision in this document is the refusal list.** An
  item never made has no end of life — a stronger claim than any leaf on a swing
  tag, and a true one.

**What we do not say** without documentation somebody has actually read:
*recycled*, *recyclable*, *eco*, *sustainable*, *biodegradable*, *carbon
neutral*, or any percentage. A woven polyester bag with polyester webbing and a
plastic cord lock is a mixed-material object, and calling it recyclable is a claim
about a waste system we do not operate. **No recycling symbol, resin code or leaf
goes into artwork** — a resin code is a statement the manufacturer makes about
their own material, and adding it as a graphic makes it ours instead.

> **Environmental claims are regulated, and the rules differ by market.** They
> belong to that market's regulator and to legal, not to this guide, which names
> no standard it has not verified — the same position it takes on [rear
> conspicuity marking](06-physical-applications.md#night). Nigeria-first is not
> Nigeria-forever: geo and currency data are already seeded globally, so an item
> printed today may be handed over in another jurisdiction.

No take-back scheme is proposed: one we cannot operate is worse than none,
because it is a promise printed on the object making it.

## Sourcing: what a supplier confirms before a run

Nothing here is guessed, and none of it belongs in an email as a hex or a hope.
Every line is **TO SPECIFY**, with the method that closes it.

| What | Method |
|---|---|
| **Minimum feature figures** — ink and gap, for the actual substrate | Ask for both numbers, then **re-run the arithmetic** in [03](03-identity-system.md#minimum-sizes-for-physical-reproduction). The person running the machine outranks a brand guide |
| **Which decoration methods they run in-house** | Ask directly. A subcontracted embroiderer means the stitch-out you approved was not made on the machine that will run the job |
| **The item's stock colour range** | A **physical swatch under D50**, not a web page. This is what answers "does this object exist in a field we can use" — and it is where most items are decided |
| **Run-to-run colour tolerance, and against what** | It is judged against the **signed proof**, never a hex ([04](04-colour-and-materials.md#spot-colour)). A bag programme is a repeat programme; drift between runs is what makes twelve bags look like three brands |
| **First-run MOQ, reorder MOQ, and repeat lead time** | Ask for all three. A low first MOQ with a high repeat MOQ is a trap: it prices the pilot and then prices out the programme |
| **Ownership of the digitised stitch file and the cut file** | Neither exists yet ([08 · what does not exist](08-usage-and-legal.md#what-does-not-exist)). The first run creates them; agree in writing that they come back to us, or the second supplier starts from a redraw |
| **Substitution policy** | Written: **no material, thread, film or component change without a new proof.** The failure is a supplier quietly improving the cord lock between runs |
| **Labour and factory conditions** | An owner's decision on what standard we hold and who verifies it. This guide names no standard it has not verified |
| **Deposit terms** | Do not pay against a PDF. A deposit goes out after a **signed physical proof** exists |
| **Market labelling requirements** — fibre content, care, country of origin | The destination market's own requirement. Confirm before layout; it claims real space on a label or a swing tag, and the layout has to give it up |

## Distribution: the right object at the wrong moment is a cost

An item's job includes **when it is handed over**. Given early it is a gift, and
a gift teaches the recipient that the object is decorative.

| Item | Handed over by | At which moment | Why not earlier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laundry bag** | The driver at first pickup, or the outlet at first walk-in intake | When there is a **first order** | A bag given at signup has no order in it. The earning test applies to the moment as much as to the object |
| **Driver kit** — bag, garments | The **provider**, from their own supplier. **Not in this catalogue** | Start of work | Drivers are the provider's, and carry no mark of ours ([09](09-brand-representation.md#decided--option-a)). Shipping kit directly to a driver would make them ours in the only way a customer at a door can see |
| **Shopfront kit** | Delivered as **one complete box** on go-live day | Go-live | A partial kit is worse than no kit: an hours plate carrying our mark, with nothing else around it, reads as a franchise sign |
| **Service card** | The field agent, at the **end** of the visit, once the business is actually registered | After registration | Handed as an opener it is a sales gift, and it makes the agent look like something they are not — see [09](09-brand-representation.md) on how a provider verifies who is standing in their shop |
| **Sample bag** | The field agent, same moment | After registration | Same reason — and unlike the card, the bag comes out of a production run. Handing one to a lead spends the run on someone who has no order to put in it |
| **Event items** | A stand hands over the **card**, not the bag | On request, to someone who asked a question | Nothing goes on a table for a stranger to take home without a reason. Bags are customer-side and expensive |

Never post merchandise to a lead, and nothing goes out carrying a market-specific
figure, a naira amount or a date: printed matter outlives the number on it, and a
stale price in someone's hand is a worse claim than no price at all.

## Before a merchandise run is committed

Run [06's eleven-line pre-production
gate](06-physical-applications.md#before-any-physical-order-ships) first — vector
artwork, height not width, clear space genuinely empty, floors re-run with the
supplier's own figures, spot code with its substrate, approved pairing, 1-bit
check, distance check, physical proof, proof signed and filed, copy through
[Verbal identity](02-verbal-identity.md). All of it applies. Merchandise adds the
lines below, because it is the only physical category **bought in quantity,
handed to strangers, and reordered**.

- [ ] **The job is written down in one sentence** — the moment in an order's life
      when someone uses it — before a quote is requested.
- [ ] **The field was chosen before the item**, and it is a row from the approved
      table.
- [ ] **The keep-clear box fits the flat panel**, not the object. Measure the
      panel between seams, curves and closures.
- [ ] **The wash test and the load test have been run on a sample** we physically
      hold.
- [ ] **The run is the smallest quantity that survives the tests**, with a repeat
      lead time in hand. If there is no repeat, this is a one-off gift, not
      merchandise — go back to the earning test.
- [ ] **No phone number, no person's name, no role claim, no date, no price.**
- [ ] **No environmental claim** that is not documented by the supplier and read
      by us.
- [ ] **Distribution moment agreed** with whoever will actually hand it over.

Sign-off is [06's two gates](06-physical-applications.md#before-any-physical-order-ships)
— brand owner on artwork, colour, contrast and size; operations owner on fitness
for purpose; both on the physical proof. Merchandise adds no third signature.
